Detailed guidance

Questions to review before scheduling

What can composite bonding change? What happens during treatment? What are the maintenance limits?

What can composite bonding change?

Composite bonding uses tooth-colored material to repair or reshape selected areas. It may be discussed for chips, edges, small gaps, or contour changes after the tooth, enamel, gums, bite, color, and treatment goals are evaluated.

What happens during treatment?

The dentist plans the shade and shape, prepares the surface as appropriate, places and shapes the material, hardens it, and checks the bite and finish. The number of visits and amount of preparation depend on the teeth and the scope of the change.

What are the maintenance limits?

Bonding can stain, wear, or chip and may need polishing, repair, or replacement. It does not whiten after placement. Ask how bonding compares with whitening, orthodontics, veneers, or a crown before choosing the treatment.

Common questions

What patients often ask before scheduling

These answers are general. Your examination and treatment plan determine what applies to you.

Can bonding change the color of a tooth?

The material can be shade matched, but it does not whiten later. Discuss whitening first when color matching matters.

Can dental bonding chip or stain?

Bonding can wear, stain, or chip. Bite forces, habits, the treated area, and daily care affect maintenance and repair needs.

How does bonding compare with veneers?

Bonding and veneers differ in material, tooth preparation, repair, color stability, treatment scope, and cost. An examination helps compare them.
