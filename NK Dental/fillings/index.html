Detailed guidance

Questions to review before scheduling

When may a filling be appropriate? How is a tooth-colored filling placed? What happens after the filling?

When may a filling be appropriate?

A filling restores an area after decay or damaged tooth structure is removed. The size, depth, location, bite, symptoms, and remaining tooth determine whether a filling is sufficient or whether another restoration or treatment should be considered.

How is a tooth-colored filling placed?

The dentist isolates the area, removes affected material as appropriate, prepares the tooth, places the selected restorative material, shapes it, and checks the bite and contacts. Material and technique depend on the tooth and clinical conditions.

What happens after the filling?

Follow the instructions for eating, numbness, cleaning, and temporary sensitivity. Fillings still need monitoring and may eventually wear, fracture, leak, or require repair or replacement. Call if pain, swelling, a high bite, or a damaged restoration concerns you.

Common questions

What patients often ask before scheduling

These answers are general. Your examination and treatment plan determine what applies to you.

How does a dentist decide whether a filling is enough?

The size, depth, location, bite, remaining tooth structure, and signs of pulpal involvement help determine whether a filling or another restoration is appropriate.

Can a filling need replacement later?

Yes. Fillings can wear, fracture, leak, or develop decay at an edge, so routine monitoring remains important.

When should I call after a filling?

Follow your written instructions and call if pain, swelling, a high bite, or a damaged restoration concerns you.
