Detailed guidance

Questions to review before scheduling

Why are professional cleanings part of preventive care? What happens at a cleaning visit? How often should I schedule care?

Why are professional cleanings part of preventive care?

Professional cleaning removes plaque and hardened deposits that home care may not fully remove. The visit also gives the dental team an opportunity to review gum condition, home-care challenges, and changes that may need a separate examination or treatment plan.

What happens at a cleaning visit?

The appropriate cleaning type depends on gum health and previous findings. A routine cleaning, periodontal maintenance visit, and scaling and root planing are not interchangeable. Ask what service is recommended, why, and whether examination, imaging, polishing, or fluoride is included for your visit.

How often should I schedule care?

Frequency depends on oral-health risk, gum condition, age, treatment history, and the dentist's recommendation. Patients who are pregnant or managing medical conditions should coordinate care with their dental and medical teams and follow product-specific instructions after preventive treatments.

Common questions

What patients often ask before scheduling

These answers are general. Your examination and treatment plan determine what applies to you.

How often should I have a professional cleaning?

Frequency depends on gum health, risk, treatment history, home care, and the dentist's recommendation rather than one schedule for everyone.

What is the difference between a routine cleaning and a deep cleaning?

They address different gum conditions and are not interchangeable. Examination and periodontal findings determine which service is appropriate.

Can I receive dental care during pregnancy?

Dental care should be coordinated with the dental and prenatal care teams based on individual needs, timing, and the treatment being considered.
