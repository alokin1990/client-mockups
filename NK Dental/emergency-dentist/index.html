02

Protect the area

Rinse gently, use a cold compress for swelling, avoid chewing there, and keep a knocked-out tooth moist while arranging care.

Call 911 or go to an emergency department for airway trouble, uncontrolled bleeding, major trauma, high fever, or spreading swelling.

Detailed guidance

Questions to review before scheduling

Which symptoms need urgent attention? What should I do before I arrive? What happens at an urgent dental visit?

Which symptoms need urgent attention?

Severe or worsening pain, facial swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, dental trauma, a knocked-out tooth, or signs of spreading infection deserve prompt guidance. Trouble breathing or swallowing, major facial trauma, or other life-threatening symptoms require emergency medical care rather than waiting for a dental appointment.

What should I do before I arrive?

Call the office so the team can assess current availability and give case-specific instructions. For a knocked-out permanent tooth, handle the crown rather than the root, keep it moist, and seek urgent care. For other problems, avoid improvised repairs and follow label directions for any nonprescription medication.

What happens at an urgent dental visit?

The immediate goal is to identify the cause, check for medical red flags, and explain stabilization and definitive treatment options. Examination, imaging, treatment, referral, timing, and relief vary with the diagnosis and available services. Ask what can be completed that day and what requires follow-up.

Common questions

What patients often ask before scheduling

These answers are general. Your examination and treatment plan determine what applies to you.

Can the office guarantee a same-day appointment?

No. Call the office to describe the problem and confirm current urgent availability. Life-threatening symptoms require emergency medical care.

Can every problem be completed during the urgent visit?

No. The visit may focus on diagnosis and stabilization, with definitive treatment or referral scheduled according to the findings and available services.

What should I bring?

Bring current health and medication information, insurance details if you plan to use benefits, and any loose restoration or tooth fragment if available.
