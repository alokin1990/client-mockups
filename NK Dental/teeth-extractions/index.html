Detailed guidance

Questions to review before scheduling

Why might a tooth be removed? What can change the procedure? How should I plan for recovery?

Why might a tooth be removed?

Removal may be considered when a tooth cannot reasonably be restored, infection or damage is significant, gum or bone support is inadequate, positioning creates a treatment problem, or another clinical plan requires space. The diagnosis should also explain preservation and observation alternatives when relevant.

What can change the procedure?

Tooth position, roots, surrounding structures, imaging, health history, medications, infection, and comfort needs affect the approach and whether referral is appropriate. Procedure length and available comfort options must be confirmed for the individual case.

How should I plan for recovery?

Use the written instructions for bleeding control, food, fluids, hygiene, activity, tobacco, medication, and warning signs. Initial recovery and longer healing vary. Ask when to call, whether follow-up is needed, and whether grafting or tooth replacement should be discussed.

Common questions

What patients often ask before scheduling

These answers are general. Your examination and treatment plan determine what applies to you.

Is extraction the only option for a damaged tooth?

Not always. The diagnosis should explain whether restorative, root canal, periodontal, observation, or removal options are relevant.

How long does recovery take?

Recovery varies with the tooth, procedure, health factors, and aftercare. Use the written instructions and timing provided for your case.

What should I ask about replacing the tooth?

Ask whether leaving the space, a bridge, denture, implant, or grafting consideration is relevant and when that decision should be made.
