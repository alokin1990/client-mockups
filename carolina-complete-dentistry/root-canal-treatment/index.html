Understand your options

What root canal treatment does

Inside a tooth is soft tissue called pulp. When it becomes inflamed or infected, root canal treatment removes the affected tissue. The canals are cleaned, shaped, disinfected, filled, and sealed.

The goal is to treat the problem inside a tooth that can still be restored. An examination helps determine whether root canal care is appropriate and what should protect the tooth afterward.

When to arrange an examination

  • Tooth pain when biting or chewing
  • Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold
  • Swelling or tenderness around a tooth
  • A damaged tooth or a change around an old filling or crown

These concerns can have different causes. Symptoms alone do not tell you which treatment you need.

Saving and restoring the tooth

The goal is a tooth you can keep using

When the tooth can be restored

Care inside, then protection outside

Root canal treatment addresses the affected tissue inside the tooth. A filling, bonding, crown, or another restoration may then be needed to reinforce its structure and support its function and appearance.

The right restoration depends on how much tooth remains and the findings at your visit. Ask about dental crowns and restorative care, including any temporary protection and the next appointment.

When the plan needs to change

Discuss the limits and alternatives

Not every tooth can be predictably restored. Damage, a fracture, or persistent symptoms can change the recommendation. The dentist may discuss further evaluation, referral, or tooth extraction, with replacement options considered separately.

A tooth that has already had root canal treatment also needs an examination if symptoms return. The examination helps you understand whether keeping the tooth is a suitable option.

Your treatment, explained

From the first examination to follow-up

At your first visit, Dr. Patel reviews your symptoms and dental history, examines the affected tooth, and discusses the findings and next steps.

  1. Examination and diagnosis

    Dr. Patel reviews your dental and medical history, symptoms, and concerns. Examination and imaging help assess the tooth and surrounding structures. Digital X-rays or CBCT imaging may be selected when appropriate for the case.

  2. A plan you understand

    Discuss the available options, comfort considerations, expected appointments, possible restorative needs, and the estimate before proceeding. Ask questions about what is included and whether another provider or referral may be needed.

  3. Cleaning and sealing the canals

    Under local anesthesia, the affected pulp is removed. The canals are cleaned, shaped, disinfected, and sealed with a biocompatible filling material. The dentist explains any temporary protection and what comes next.

  4. Restoring the tooth

    A crown or another restoration may be recommended to protect the treated tooth and support function and appearance. Discuss the type of restoration, who will complete it, and its timing. Canal treatment and the final restoration are distinct parts of the plan.

  5. Aftercare and follow-up

    You receive instructions about discomfort, eating, cleaning, and any recommended medication. Follow the team's directions for chewing while the tooth is temporarily protected. Continue brushing, flossing, and dental checkups as advised, and attend the planned follow-up visits.

    Contact the office about worsening or unexpected symptoms, a problem with the temporary restoration, or questions about recovery. Follow-up gives the team a chance to monitor healing and adjust care when needed.

Before your appointment

Questions before your visit

Bring your questions about the tooth, your comfort, and what happens after treatment.

What if I am worried about pain or dental anxiety?

Tell the team before treatment. Local anesthesia is used, and Dr. Patel can discuss appropriate comfort options and what to expect. Sensations and recovery vary, so ask for instructions tailored to your case and call if symptoms concern you.

How many appointments will I need, and how long is recovery?

The tooth, canal complexity, restorative work, and your response to treatment affect the schedule and recovery. Dr. Patel can explain how many visits to expect, when the final restoration can be placed, and what recovery may involve for your tooth.

Will I need a crown after a root canal?

A crown or another restoration may be recommended to protect the tooth. The choice depends on its remaining structure and function. Ask which restoration is planned, who will complete it, and how to protect the tooth until that work is finished.

What if a tooth that already had a root canal starts hurting?

Contact the office for an examination. Previous treatment and new symptoms need to be reviewed together. The dentist can explain the findings and whether further assessment, another treatment approach, or a referral should be considered.

How should I care for the tooth afterward?

Follow the specific eating, medication, cleaning, and follow-up instructions you receive. Continue brushing, flossing, and regular dental visits as advised. Call if discomfort worsens, the temporary restoration changes, or you are unsure about the next step.

What should I ask about cost and insurance?

Ask whether the estimate includes imaging, canal treatment, and the final filling or crown. Confirm your plan’s benefits and expected share with the team before care. See cost and payment information.

Are financing or membership options available?

SunBit and Proceed Finance are available; approval and terms depend on the provider and your application. The practice also offers a membership plan, which is not dental insurance. Ask about current eligibility and what is included.
