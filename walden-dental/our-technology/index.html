Walden Dental uses digital dental technology in Austin to help the dental team examine, explain, and plan care.

Digital radiographs

Images selected for a clinical question

Walden Dental uses Dentrix digital radiographs to examine areas that may not be visible during a clinical examination and to support diagnosis and treatment planning.

Your dentist decides when radiographs may be useful based on the tooth, symptoms, oral health, history, and the question the visit needs to answer. That means the type and timing of images can differ from one patient or visit to another.

Clinical selection comes first. Digital equipment provides information. The examination and the dentist's interpretation determine how that information fits your care.

A closer chairside view

See what the intraoral camera shows

A small intraoral camera can create enlarged color images of areas inside the mouth.

Dr. Frank can review those images with you while explaining a finding or discussing treatment choices. The camera is a viewing and communication tool; it is not a digital impression scanner and does not determine a treatment on its own.

In-office CAD/CAM

CEREC for selected restorative care

CEREC combines digital design software with in-office equipment for selected same-day crowns and applicable restorative or cosmetic cases.

The tooth, material, treatment plan, and clinical needs determine whether this workflow is appropriate and whether care can be completed in one visit.

Read about same-day crowns, dental veneers, or cosmetic dentistry.

Precision dentistry

Electric handpieces for selected care

Walden Dental uses electric handpieces during applicable restorative and cosmetic procedures, including selected crown and veneer preparation.

The handpiece is one part of the procedure. The condition of the tooth, the planned restoration, and the dentist's clinical judgment guide how the visit is carried out.

See general patient FAQs
Does every patient need the same dental imaging?

No. The examination and clinical question guide whether radiographs or another documented tool may be useful for a particular visit.

What can an intraoral camera show?

The small camera can create enlarged color images of areas inside the mouth. Dr. Frank can review those images with patients while explaining findings and choices.

Does CEREC mean every crown is completed in one visit?

No. Walden Dental uses CEREC for selected same-day crown and applicable restorative or cosmetic cases. The tooth, treatment plan, and clinical needs determine whether that workflow is appropriate.

Can I book an appointment for a specific technology?

Book a dental visit or call the Austin office and describe what brings you in. The dental team can explain which information or tools may be useful after reviewing your needs.
