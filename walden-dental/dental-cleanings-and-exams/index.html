What a cleaning does

A fresh start for your teeth and gums

A professional cleaning removes plaque and hardened deposits that brushing and flossing at home cannot fully remove. Your visit also gives the team a chance to check for changes in your teeth and gums.

Bleeding, tenderness, sensitivity, or a long gap since your last visit are worth discussing. They do not tell you which kind of cleaning you need on their own.

Due for a checkup?

Ask for a dental visit even if nothing hurts. A dentist can find concerns that are not obvious at home.

It has been a while?

There is no need to diagnose yourself before calling. Share when you last came in and any changes you have noticed.

Want a brighter smile?

Cleaning may remove surface deposits. It is different from teeth whitening, which addresses tooth color.

The right kind of care

Routine cleaning or gum treatment?

The recommendation depends on gum health and the deposits found. A deeper procedure is not an upgrade to a routine cleaning; it addresses a different need.

Routine cleaning

A preventive cleaning focuses on deposits on the teeth when an examination supports routine care.

Periodontal treatment

When gum findings show disease, the dentist may discuss treatment below the gumline instead. The team can explain why.

Future visits

Your return interval depends on gum health, cavity risk, health history, home care, and what the team finds. It is not the same for everyone.

At your appointment

What happens at a cleaning and exam

New and returning visits may differ. The team can explain which services are recommended and whether imaging or additional care is needed.

  1. Share your history

    Tell the team about health changes, concerns, and sensitive areas.

  2. Check teeth and gums

    An exam helps distinguish routine care from concerns that need another plan. Digital imaging may be used when clinically indicated.

  3. Clean as appropriate

    The hygiene visit addresses deposits and surface buildup according to the findings and recommended procedure.

  4. Discuss findings

    Dr. Frank and the team can explain areas to watch and any treatment worth considering.

  5. Plan home care

    Ask about brushing, cleaning between teeth, and what to do if you notice sensitivity afterward.

  6. Set the next visit

    Follow-up timing is based on your needs, not a fixed rule for everyone.

    Cleanings and exams FAQs

    Questions before you book

    Your examination determines which services are appropriate for your visit.

    How will I know which type of cleaning I need?

    The team assesses your teeth and gums before recommending routine cleaning or another form of care. Symptoms alone cannot identify the procedure.

    Are X-rays part of every visit?

    Not necessarily. Imaging is selected according to your history, prior records, concerns, and examination findings. Ask the office what is planned.

    How long should I allow for the appointment?

    Visit length depends on the exam, cleaning type, imaging needs, and findings to discuss. The office can advise you when scheduling.

    How often should I come back?

    Your dentist or hygienist will recommend an interval based on your teeth, gums, health history, and risk factors rather than one schedule for everyone.

    What if I feel sensitive afterward?

    Contact the office if discomfort is severe, persists, or concerns you. The team can advise you based on your visit.
