How whitening works

A brighter shade starts with the right assessment

Professional whitening uses peroxide-based gel to lighten discoloration in natural teeth. Coffee, tea, wine, tobacco, food dyes and some medications can contribute to color changes, but the cause and response are different for each person.

Whitening is different from a cleaning: a hygienist removes surface deposits and plaque, while bleaching changes tooth color. An examination helps Dr. Frank decide whether whitening, cleaning or another approach is worth discussing.

What whitening cannot change

Fillings, bonding, crowns, bridges, veneers and dentures do not bleach like natural teeth. Existing dental work may therefore appear different after surrounding teeth lighten. Dr. Frank can review your restorations, tooth and gum health, sensitivity, and the kind of discoloration before you decide how to proceed.

Your whitening choices

In-office Zoom or custom trays?

Both options are offered at Walden Dental. The useful difference is how treatment fits your goals, schedule, and sensitivity after an evaluation. Shade changes vary, so Dr. Frank can help set realistic expectations for your teeth.

At the office

Philips Zoom whitening

For selected patients, the team applies the Zoom gel to clean teeth, protects surrounding soft tissues, and uses a light to activate the gel. The number of applications and shade response vary.

Dr. Frank assesses whether this office-based approach suits your teeth and gums before treatment.

At home

Custom take-home trays

The team creates trays designed to fit your mouth, provides whitening gel, and shows you how to fill and wear the trays according to your individual instructions.

This offers more control over when you use them, while your dentist still guides the plan and checks whether whitening fits your needs.

If the concern involves a restoration or color that may not respond to bleaching, ask about veneers or an appropriate restoration plan. A cleaning may help with surface deposits; these are different decisions from bleaching.

From evaluation to maintenance

What happens before and after whitening?

Dr. Frank begins with your shade goals, oral health, sensitivity, and existing restorations before recommending a whitening route.

  1. First, examine

    Review the source of discoloration, teeth and gums, existing dental work, past whitening and sensitivity. A plan can account for whether cleaning or other care should come first.

  2. Then, choose

    For in-office Zoom, the team protects nearby tissues before applying the gel and activating it with light. For home whitening, the team fits custom trays and explains how to use the supplied gel.

  3. Afterward, adjust

    Temporary tooth sensitivity or gum discomfort can occur. Contact the office if you have concerns so the team can advise you about your individual instructions or adjustments.

  4. Maintain the result

    Whitening is not permanent. Staining habits, oral hygiene, check-ups, and possible touch-ups can affect how your smile looks over time; the interval varies by person.

Teeth-whitening questions

Answers to discuss at your visit

These answers are general; an examination and personal instructions determine the right plan for you.

Can I benefit from teeth whitening?

If you want to discuss stained or discolored natural teeth, Dr. Frank can assess the cause and your oral health to see whether professional whitening may fit.

Does teeth whitening work on dental restorations?

No. Fillings, bonding, crowns, bridges, dentures and veneers do not bleach like natural teeth. Your dentist can discuss how their color may affect a whitening plan.

How long can I expect my teeth to remain white after treatment?

Whitening is not permanent. Food and drink habits, smoking, hygiene and follow-up care can affect how long a change remains visible; there is no fixed interval for everyone.

Will whitening damage my teeth?

Professional care starts with an assessment of your teeth and gums and use instructions suited to you. Sensitivity or tissue irritation can still occur, so discuss concerns or prior reactions before treatment.

Does teeth whitening hurt?

Some people notice temporary tooth sensitivity or gum discomfort during or after whitening. Let the team know about sensitive teeth so they can advise you about your options and when to contact them.
