How dental implants work

A replacement designed from the root up

A dental implant is a titanium post placed in the jaw to support a replacement tooth. After the site has healed as planned, an abutment and custom restoration complete the visible part of the tooth.

Missing teeth can affect chewing, speech, neighboring tooth position, and the bone that once supported the root. The timing and degree of those changes vary, which is why an evaluation matters.

Compare your choices

Implant, bridge, or denture?

No single option fits every patient. The right path depends on the number and location of missing teeth, the condition of nearby teeth and bone, whether you prefer a fixed or removable restoration, timing, maintenance, and cost.

01

Fixed replacement

Dental implant

An implant supports a crown without using neighboring teeth as anchors. It requires a surgical phase, healing and monitoring, then restoration.

  • Consider available bone and health factors
  • Plan for surgical and restorative phases
  • Maintain with daily care and professional visits
02

Fixed replacement

Dental bridge

A bridge spans a missing-tooth space and is supported by neighboring teeth or other planned supports.

  • Review the condition of adjacent teeth
  • Compare cleaning needs around the bridge
  • Discuss how the bridge fits your bite
03

Removable replacement

Partial or full denture

A removable denture can replace several teeth or a complete arch, depending on the design and remaining oral structures.

  • Consider fit, stability, and adaptation
  • Review daily removal and cleaning
  • Plan routine fit and tissue checks

Dental implant questions

Answers before you book

An evaluation is the only way to determine which option and sequence fit your situation. These answers explain what is commonly discussed.

What are dental implants made of?

Dental implants are commonly made from titanium, a biocompatible material designed to integrate with supporting bone. The visible restoration may use ceramic or other dental materials selected for the plan.

Am I a candidate for a dental implant?

Candidacy depends on oral health, available bone, health history, medications, healing factors, and the planned restoration. Dr. Frank evaluates these factors before recommending a path.

What if I do not have enough bone?

Bone grafting may be discussed when the site does not provide the support needed for the proposed plan. Whether it is appropriate, and when it would occur, depends on the individual evaluation.

How long does dental implant treatment take?

The course varies with the site, healing response, grafting needs, and restoration. Your plan should outline expected phases and checkpoints after the evaluation.

Will the procedure hurt?

The team discusses local anesthesia, comfort planning, what you may feel during treatment, and postoperative instructions. Experiences vary, so ask about any concern before treatment.

How do I care for an implant?

Daily brushing, cleaning between the implant and neighboring teeth, and professional cleanings and examinations help the team monitor the implant, restoration, gums, and bite.

Does insurance cover dental implants?

Coverage depends on your plan and the services included in your case. Walden Dental can help you review an estimate, but benefits should be verified before treatment.
