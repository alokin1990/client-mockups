The first decision

What can veneers change?

A veneer is a thin, tooth-colored covering bonded to the front of a visible tooth. Dr. Frank may use it to change the appearance of a stain, chip, small gap, irregular contour, or worn edge. A crown covers more of a tooth; direct bonding applies resin to the tooth instead of attaching a separately made shell.

Your first visit is an examination, not a promise that veneers are the right answer. Dr. Frank examines your teeth, gums, enamel and bite, then discusses the changes you want before recommending a veneer plan. Grinding, active decay or gum concerns, severe alignment, and the amount of healthy enamel may change the recommendation.

Material and preparation

Porcelain, composite, and the trade-offs

Porcelain and composite resin are two veneer materials to discuss. Porcelain can mimic enamel's translucency and resist staining; composite resin is another tooth-colored option. Their fit, repair possibilities, maintenance and visit sequence differ. Ask which materials Walden Dental offers for your case and why.

Porcelain

Custom ceramic appearance

Walden Dental describes porcelain veneers as customized ceramic shells. For selected cases, its in-office CEREC equipment can help design and make them during one visit. That timing is not appropriate for every tooth or plan.

Composite resin

Another material to discuss

Composite resin may be another option. Dr. Frank can explain whether direct bonding, composite veneers, or ceramic veneers fit your goal after examining your teeth.

Understand preparation before deciding

A veneer may require removing enamel to make room for the restoration. When enamel is removed, that change is generally irreversible. Veneers also do not treat underlying decay or gum disease, and they can chip, loosen, wear, or need repair or replacement over time. Severe bite or alignment concerns may call for another approach.

From examination to upkeep

How your veneer plan takes shape

Your evaluation guides the preparation, material, timing, and aftercare for your individual veneer plan.

  1. Evaluate and design

    Discuss shade, length, shape and the tooth or teeth that concern you. Dr. Frank reviews gum and tooth health, enamel, bite, photographs or scans as needed, alternatives, the proposed sequence, and an estimate.

  2. Prepare when needed

    Some plans require enamel preparation before a veneer is made. Your dentist explains the amount of change and whether a temporary restoration or more than one visit is expected before you agree.

  3. Make, try in, and bond

    Selected cases may use Walden Dental's in-office CEREC design and fabrication. Other plans may use different timing. The veneer is checked for appearance and fit, bonded, and the bite is reviewed.

  4. Care for it

    Brush, floss, and keep regular dental visits. Avoid habits that can chip a restoration. If you grind your teeth, discuss whether a nightguard or splint is appropriate. Call if a veneer feels loose, chips, or your bite changes.

Questions about veneers

Questions before your visit

Your examination and written plan determine what applies to your teeth.

What are dental veneers?

Veneers are thin tooth-colored coverings bonded to the visible front surfaces of teeth. They may change color, shape, spacing or the appearance of wear after a dentist checks whether the underlying tooth is healthy enough.

What are veneers made of?

Veneers may be made from porcelain ceramic or composite resin. Porcelain can be chosen for a natural enamel-like appearance and stain resistance; the best choice depends on the tooth and plan.

Could veneers help with my concern?

They may be discussed for chips, worn edges, discoloration, small spaces or irregular front-tooth contours. An examination of enamel, gums, bite and any grinding habits is needed before Dr. Frank can recommend them.

Can the veneer procedure be reversed?

When enamel is removed to fit a veneer, that change is generally irreversible. Ask what preparation your individual plan requires before deciding.

How long do veneers last?

There is no guaranteed lifespan. Material, bite, habits, hygiene and follow-up affect wear; a veneer may need repair or replacement. If you grind your teeth, ask whether a protective appliance is appropriate.
