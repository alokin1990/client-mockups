Start with your goals

What would you like your smile to do for you?

A smile makeover may address appearance, but tooth structure, gum health, and your bite matter too. An examination helps separate cosmetic preferences from needs that should be addressed first.

Color and finish

Stains, old bonding, or uneven color may prompt a conversation about whitening or restorations.

Shape and spacing

Chips, wear, gaps, and uneven contours can be discussed alongside the amount of healthy tooth structure.

Function and missing teeth

Broken, aching, or missing teeth may call for restorative care before or alongside appearance changes.

A plan that fits real life

Visits, travel, budget, and maintenance can influence which treatments are sequenced and when.

Smiles with their own stories

Four patients. Four different plans.

These are individual patient stories. Treatment plans, timing, and results vary. Before-and-after photographs show these patients, not a prediction of your result.

Patient story · Clemente

Worn enamel and failing fillings

Clemente was concerned about deteriorating enamel and fillings. He wanted one dentist to coordinate cosmetic and surgical work while respecting his time and budget.

His individual plan included extractions, implant placement, healing bridges, and bite rehabilitation. Dr. Frank described the aim as protecting the jaw joints and helping him chew while treatment progressed.

Clemente reported clearer speech and more confidence smiling and speaking during sales presentations. These are his personal experiences, not a promised result.

Patient story · Chris

A complex, multi-treatment plan

Chris needed extensive care involving root canals, extractions, bridges, implants, crowns, and veneers. His care involved two intensive treatment days; that timing was specific to him.

The first day addressed upper teeth with root canals, crowns, bridges, and extractions. The next day involved lower-tooth implants, extractions, root canals, and crowns.

His recovery required commitment. Dr. Frank describes the case as challenging and fulfilling, and Chris's experience illustrates how a coordinated plan can bring several kinds of care together.

Patient story · Blair

Rebuilding after years of damage

Blair had lived with fractured, chipped, and worn teeth for more than 15 years. Earlier dental experiences had made him reluctant to seek care, even as oral health and appearance affected daily life and work.

His individual plan required one extraction along with cavity removal, gum contouring, lower-jaw implants, and a combination of crowns, bridges, and veneers for upper and lower teeth.

Blair described improvements in comfort, chewing, confidence, and energy after his care. Those observations belong to his individual story, not a prediction for someone else.

Patient story · Sara

A plan that accounted for travel and bite concerns

Repeated bonding failures led Sara to seek stronger restorations and a balanced bite. She was also concerned about infections affecting her teeth, gums, and jawbone.

After moving to Florida, she needed a dentist who could account for travel, location, schedule, and budget. Walden Dental planned her care around those circumstances.

Her treatment included upper and lower veneers and crowns. Dr. Frank describes her new appearance as fresh and natural; her result is an individual example, not a standard outcome.

A coordinated approach

Several options can serve one goal

A makeover is not a fixed package. Your plan may combine cosmetic changes with treatment that restores support, comfort, or chewing function. The sequence depends on the examination and what matters to you.

  1. Begin with health

    Review decay, gums, existing restorations, missing teeth, and the bite before choosing an appearance change.

  2. Compare treatments

    Bonding, veneers, whitening, tooth-colored fillings, crowns, bridges, implants, and gum contouring address different needs.

  3. Sequence the care

    Some steps may be phased around healing, travel, budget, or other treatment. Not every plan needs every procedure.

Your first conversation

A plan starts with understanding your smile

At your consultation, Dr. Frank reviews your concerns, examines your teeth, gums, and bite, and discusses the options and sequence that may make sense for you.

Share your priorities. Tell the team what you want to change and what has not worked before.

Review your oral health. Dr. Frank may order digital X-rays or other imaging when needed to understand what an unaided view cannot show.

Compare routes and timing. Discuss treatments, alternatives, a possible phased schedule, upkeep, and a plan-specific estimate before deciding.

    Smile makeover FAQs

    Questions to bring to your visit

    The right combination and timeline depend on your oral health, goals, and treatment plan.

    See general patient FAQs
    What treatments can a smile makeover include?

    A plan may include two or more approaches such as porcelain veneers, crowns, bridges, bonding, tooth-colored fillings, whitening, gum contouring, or care for missing teeth. The examination determines which are appropriate for you.

    How long can a smile makeover take?

    Timing depends on the type and number of procedures and how much change or healing is needed. One person may have a short course; another may need multiple visits or choose to space care over weeks or months. The two-day course in Chris's story was individual, not a standard schedule.

    Are smile makeovers costly?

    Cost varies with the treatments, materials, and phases in the individual plan. Ask for a written estimate and discuss any scheduling or payment considerations before you begin.

    Does insurance cover smile makeovers?

    Coverage depends on your plan and the purpose of each proposed treatment. Confirm participation and benefits with the office and your insurer for your plan.

    Am I a candidate for a smile makeover?

    An examination is needed to decide which options may help. Tooth decay, gum disease, or other oral-health concerns may need attention before appearance-focused treatment.
