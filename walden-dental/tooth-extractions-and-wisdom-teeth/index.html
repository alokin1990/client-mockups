Start with the concern

When should you call?

Tooth pain does not automatically mean an extraction. A cracked tooth, extensive decay, a loose tooth, swelling, or a wisdom tooth that is causing trouble can each call for a different response. An exam helps identify what is happening.

Do not wait on spreading swelling. Call for prompt dental advice. If swelling makes it hard to breathe or swallow, seek emergency medical help.

Pain or swelling

These can have more than one cause. Explain when symptoms began, whether they are changing, and any fever or swelling when you call.

A broken or heavily restored tooth

The amount of healthy tooth left, the bite, and surrounding structures matter when comparing a repair with removal.

A wisdom tooth concern

Position, eruption, nearby teeth, and symptoms can affect whether monitoring, treatment, or a specialist opinion is appropriate.

Understand the choices

Can the tooth be saved?

The answer depends on an examination. The dentist may consider the remaining tooth structure, infection, gum support, tooth position, health history, and what you want from treatment.

Preserve the tooth

A filling or crown may be considered when enough healthy structure remains. If the inner tissue is affected, the dentist can discuss whether additional treatment or a referral could preserve it.

How crown care is evaluated

Consider removal

If the tooth cannot be restored predictably, extraction may become one option. The clinician must explain whether the situation is straightforward or more complex before a procedure is planned.

Seek a specialist opinion

Impacted wisdom teeth, difficult roots, health factors, or other findings may call for an oral-surgery referral. Ask who would provide each part of your care.

If removal is recommended, replacement is a separate decision. Depending on the tooth and your goals, the conversation may include an implant, bridge, removable option, or no replacement for a wisdom tooth.

What happens at the visit

A decision before a procedure

Expect the team to review your concern and health history. Examination and imaging, when needed, help the dentist explain the choices and whether a referral is appropriate.

  1. Tell us what changed

    Describe symptoms, past treatment, medications, and any medical conditions relevant to dental care.

  2. Examine the tooth

    The dentist checks the tooth, surrounding gum and bone, and bite. Imaging may be recommended to see structures that are not visible.

  3. Compare options

    Discuss whether the tooth can be restored, what removal might involve, or whether a specialist should evaluate it.

  4. Review the estimate

    Ask which visits, imaging, procedure, and follow-up items are included before accepting a plan.

  5. Receive individual instructions

    Preparation and recovery advice depend on the treatment actually chosen and your health. Follow the clinician's written directions.

  6. Know when to call

    If an extraction occurs, contact the treating clinician about worsening pain, bleeding, swelling, or anything outside the instructions you were given.

Dry socket is one possible complication after extraction. If pain worsens after the procedure, notify the dentist rather than trying to diagnose the socket yourself.

Common questions

Questions before you decide

These answers help you prepare for a conversation. Your own exam and the treating clinician's instructions guide care.

Does tooth pain mean the tooth has to be removed?

No. Pain can have different causes. The dentist needs to examine the tooth and surrounding structures before recommending a repair, another treatment, removal, or referral.

Are wisdom teeth always removed?

No. Whether a wisdom tooth should be monitored, treated, or referred depends on its position, symptoms, neighboring teeth, and clinical findings.

Can a tooth be removed during the evaluation?

Do not assume a procedure can happen at the first visit. The findings, health history, provider scope, and scheduling all affect the next step. Call the office to discuss your situation.

What is dry socket?

It is a painful condition that can occur if the clot protecting an extraction site is disrupted. If pain gets worse after removal, contact the treating dentist for advice.

Will I need to replace the tooth?

That depends on which tooth it is and the broader treatment plan. A wisdom tooth and a tooth used for chewing or appearance may lead to different conversations. Ask about the choices before removal.

More to discuss

Care depends on the tooth

A tooth with enough sound structure might be restored with a crown. If a tooth is missing or cannot be kept, implant planning may be one later conversation. Your dentist can explain which questions apply to you.
