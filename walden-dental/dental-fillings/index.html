Start with an examination

Is a filling the right repair?

A dental filling replaces a limited area of lost tooth structure. It may be considered for a cavity, small break, or failing restoration. The dentist first needs to assess the tooth, its bite, and how much sound structure remains.

Do not wait for pain to decide whether a tooth needs attention: decay can be found at a checkup before obvious symptoms appear.

Sensitivity or a rough edge

Describe when it started and what triggers it. Those details help the examination, but they do not identify the cause by themselves.

An old filling has changed

Tell the team if a filling feels loose, chipped, or different when you bite. The tooth and existing material should be examined before repair or replacement.

A cavity found at a visit

Ask how much of the tooth is affected and why a direct filling, a crown, or another choice is recommended.

Compare the choices

A filling is one way to restore a tooth

Location, extent of damage, remaining tooth structure, bite forces, and appearance all affect the recommendation. Walden Dental describes tooth-colored composite fillings; the dentist can confirm the options appropriate for your tooth.

Direct filling

Composite resin is placed into the prepared area and shaped to restore a limited portion of the tooth. It does not cover the whole tooth.

Crown

When more coverage is needed, a dental crown may be discussed. Selected cases may use Walden Dental's CEREC workflow.

Another plan

A tooth may need monitoring or another type of care, depending on its condition. The examination is where alternatives, timing, and cost are explained.

At your appointment

What happens during filling care

The exact steps depend on the tooth and the restoration selected. The office can explain comfort measures, materials, and aftercare for your plan.

  1. Examine the tooth

    Dr. Frank reviews the concern, the tooth and bite, and imaging when indicated.

  2. Discuss the choice

    Ask why a filling fits, which material is planned, and whether another restoration is more appropriate.

  3. Prepare the area

    When a filling is selected, the damaged or decayed area is treated before restorative material is placed.

  4. Place the restoration

    The filling is shaped and the bite is checked before the visit ends.

  5. Review instructions

    Ask when to eat, what sensations to expect, and whether your plan needs follow-up.

  6. Call about changes

    Contact the office if a filling breaks, your bite feels uneven, or discomfort concerns you.

    Dental fillings FAQs

    Questions to ask about your tooth

    A diagnosis and written plan depend on your examination.

    How will I know if I need a filling?

    Dr. Frank examines the tooth and may use imaging when indicated. The amount and location of damage help determine whether a direct filling or another option fits.

    What material does Walden Dental use?

    Walden Dental describes tooth-colored composite fillings. Ask the office which materials are available and recommended for your specific tooth.

    Can an old filling be repaired or replaced?

    That depends on the filling and the tooth beneath it. Have a changed or damaged restoration examined before deciding on repair or replacement.

    When can I eat after treatment?

    Follow the instructions given for your restoration and any anesthetic used. The team can tell you what applies to your visit.

    What if a filling breaks or falls out?

    Call Walden Dental to arrange an assessment. Avoid trying to reattach it yourself, and tell the team about pain, swelling, or trouble biting.
